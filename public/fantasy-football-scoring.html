Fantasy football scoring explained

Fantasy football converts real football statistics into league points. The exact values depend on league settings, so understanding the scoring system is one of the first steps in evaluating players correctly.

Standard, half-PPR and PPR

Reception scoring changes the value of pass catchers. Standard formats usually award no points simply for a catch, half-PPR adds half a point per reception, and PPR adds one point per reception. That difference can materially change running back, wide receiver and tight end value.

Quarterback scoring

Quarterbacks commonly score through passing yards, passing touchdowns, rushing yards and rushing touchdowns, with deductions for interceptions in many leagues. Because scoring settings differ, the same quarterback performance can produce different fantasy totals from league to league.

Skill-position scoring

Running backs, wide receivers and tight ends typically earn points for rushing and receiving yards, touchdowns and receptions when the format includes PPR scoring. Fumbles may create deductions.

Kickers and defenses

Kicker scoring often depends on field-goal distance and extra points. Defense/special teams scoring commonly includes sacks, interceptions, fumble recoveries, defensive touchdowns and points allowed.

Why settings matter

  • Reception scoring changes target volume value.
  • Passing-touchdown values change quarterback gaps.
  • Bonuses can reward long plays or yardage milestones.
  • Defense scoring can vary substantially across leagues.
  • Always check league settings before relying on rankings or projections.
