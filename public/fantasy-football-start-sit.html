Fantasy football start sit strategy

Start/sit decisions are easiest when you separate role, matchup and team context. One projected point total should not be the only reason a player enters or leaves your lineup.

Start with opportunity

Volume is the foundation. Snaps, routes, touches, targets and goal-line work usually matter more than a single recent fantasy score.

Use matchup as a tiebreaker

Matchups matter, but they should usually adjust a player’s expectation rather than completely override a strong role. When two options have similar usage, opponent strength can become the deciding factor.

Match risk to your matchup

If you are a heavy favorite, a stable floor can be valuable. If you are a major underdog, a higher-variance option with more big-play or touchdown upside may make more sense.

Recheck injuries and game status

Late injury news can change a player’s workload and the opportunities around him. Final lineup decisions should account for active/inactive status and changes to teammates who affect usage.

Start/sit checklist

  • Compare projected role and opportunity.
  • Check injury status and expected limitations.
  • Use matchup and game environment as context.
  • Consider floor versus ceiling based on your matchup.
  • Prefer clear volume when two options are otherwise close.
